# Hi, I'm Corin.

I’m a carpenter, project manager, and technical mentor with a background in Mathematics and Computer Science from Vassar College. I've spent my career bridging abstract problem-solving--from building tech infrastructure as a startup CTO to teaching high school math--with physical, hands-on craft. Below is the full, unabridged story of how I got here.

![Corin building a deck](/images/deck.jpg)

I love to learn, and to use that knowledge to build things and solve problems. I believe that everyone has a unique perspective on the world and deserves empathy, and that if we all put some more effort into understanding a bit more about the world and each other we would be better off.

![Me saving my cats life after it claims it hasn't been fed in decades](/images/baby_cat.png)

I was born in Schenectady, New York and grew up in Jonesboro Maine, where my family moved shortly after. Nestled into the woods along the tidal Chandler River, my parents spared me from having any other chores as long as I moved firewood from where they dumped it by our driveway around back to our wood shed and stacked it neatly through the summer so we could stay warm in the winter. The river went out with the ocean and revealed entire ecosystems, only to be hidden again a few hours later with the tides, changing slightly every cycle.

I learned a lot about self sufficiency, the importance of community and connection to the environment in rural life. I learned that I had real talent in mathematics and was very capable with computers. 

I attended the Maine School of Science and Mathematics for high school. The public boarding school focused on STEM was situated in the northern limits of the state, among vast fields of potatoes and broccoli in the summer and blankets of snow the other nine months of the year. Here I learned how to apply myself and work hard, how to build community. I developed a stronger sense of myself and my values. I learned that while I am very technically skilled, that is only part of the equation if we want to solve problems in the real world. Mathematics, technology, science all exist in the society we've created. They are all fundamentally human endeavors. They are not immune to human issues like bias, and they can all be leveraged for evil as much as they can be used for good. I developed a passion for open source software and grew concerns about digital privacy, and Bernie got me interested in politics with his run in 2016.

Maine has a special place in my heart, the vast state contains as much varied human experience as it does geography. The Mountains, the Forests, the Ocean. The rural fishing communities and blueberry barrens, the farmers, the vibrant and unique city life surrounding Portland. I did however want to broaden my experience somewhat, and so I attended Vassar College.

In the Hudson Valley in New York, I was closer to where I was born than I had ever lived, but it was all unfamiliar to me. I met people from vastly different parts of the world and lifestyles than me, and I gained a broader understanding of how the world works. This deepened my already developing view that humanity on the whole has much more in common than we have differentiating us.

I studied advanced math and computer science, and I also tried my best to spend my time outside of the classroom learning as well. I worked in Vassar's nationally renowned theatre department, first as a Theatrical Journeyman and then as the Assistant Technical Director, building sets for the main proscenium Martell stage, the black box Powerhouse Theatre, and the smaller Streep black box theatre. I worked for some incredibly talented people and learned a lot about carpentry, creative problem solving, and managing a team. 

I gained a lot of experience teaching, I challenged myself in the math department, and I participated in student government and various extracurriculars. Then, the pandemic happened!

I graduated in 2020. I'd done a pretty good job of planning out my curriculum--I could have graduated early with a Computer Science degree, but I decided to take the extra time to complete my Math major as well, which I frankly was more interested in anyway but had worse job prospects. My second semester senior year, I didn't have all that much to do. I was TA for a Drama class on Stagecraft, I worked in the scene shop, I had an indepednent study exploring Category Theory and how it connects Math and Computer Science, and I took another two classes just to maintain my full-time status. I was pretty excited to have a fun and relaxed last semester, after pushing myself pretty hard academically through high school and college.

I'd also began work on fleshing out our pitch for eBumps, an ad-tech startup my friend had come to me with first semester senior year. The idea initially was just to make digital customizable bumper stickers. We developed the idea from there to be a hyper-local digital advertising platform, where we would develop hardware and distribute it to ride-share drivers initially in Manhattan at no charge to mount on their vehicles and get paid to display ads. We developed an ad marketplace where companies could purchase ad time, targetting specific locations. For example, if a store had a sale or a new product and wanter to bring new customers in, they could have their ads be displayed on every equipped vehicle on their block to catch the attention of passers by.

We formed a corporation with my friend as the CEO and me as the CTO, and developed a prototype with a [Raspberry Pi](https://www.raspberrypi.com/products/raspberry-pi-5/) and a screen that we were able to remotely serve ads to. We pitched this idea to an investor in February of 2020, and secured an investment of $400,000 to file for a [patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20230072209A1), hire a team, and develop the product with the plan to launch a trial run the following year in Manhattan. 

I hired a talented software engineer to build out our backend, and a mobile app developer to create an app for drivers that would pair with the device and allow drivers to review their earnings, and gave them complete freedom to blacklist certain industries or specific businesses that they did not want to advertise for. We hired an engineering firm to build the hardware and our mounting system. We designed our mounting system to distinguish us from the competition such as [Firefly](https://www.fireflyon.com/), which requried the displays to be bolted onto the vehicle permanently. Since we were targetting ride-share drivers who used their personal vehicles, we wanted a system where they could easily remove the device. So, we engineered a mount that used industrial adhesive to securely affix to the vehicle, and then the device itself easily snapped into and out of this mount. Our engineering firm did extensive testing to ensure safety, and to put together instructions for driver's to remove the mount and adhesive without damaging their vehicle at any time.

I'll be honest, this was never an idea I was passionate about. I generally am not a fan of advertising. I think it can be done well, and there are aspects of eBumps that I did like: the hyper-local nature, the business model where we distributed the devices to ride-share driver's at no charge and gave them additional income and freedom to display the ads of their choosing. I was also extremely grateful for the opportunity to run a business. I got to work with brilliant software and hardware engineers to develop our hardware that used GPS and an IMU to determine which ads to show and calculate driver payouts, cellular service to communicate with our backend to receive ads and updates, and bluetooth to pair with the driver's phone. While our developers were in the weeds writing code under my direction, I took a high level view and ensured our system was ready to scale, with Ansible for managing the devices and Kubernetes for scaling our backend. This experience was so valuable and I learned a ton!

When the pandemic lockdowns began during my spring break, I ended up returning home to my parents' house in Maine to finish up the semester and keep working on this startup. To be honest, this was a pretty challenging stretch. I really felt like I lost out on the end of my college experience. I didn't have the chance to even say goodbye to all of my people. 

Like everyone else, I stayed occupied learning how to make sourdough bread and binge watching Tiger King, among other things. I continued to work on this startup, but felt more and more disconnected as time went on. When George Floyd was murdered and protests broke out across the country, I wondered what I could possibly do from my room in the woods in Maine.

The following summer, I built the garage for my parents discussed on my build portfolio--this was a really fun project. It was the first project I was really in charge of. My parents described what they wanted, and I put together an order from the local lumber yard and used whatever tools I had scavenged together. We framed it up in just a week, and took a bit more time to install the roof, the garage door, and do the finish work. I could go into plenty of detail about minor mistakes I made, but all in all it came out really well and is still standing to this day!

I continued working on eBumps and did some more small carpentry projects around the house. I also finally got a driver's license--I'd done a pretty good job getting around via public transit, and up until the pandemic would've been comfortable never getting a car. I did become pretty uncomfortable taking public transit with an ongoing pandemic, so I ended up getting my first car. Funny that I'd later live in a van for almost 2 years!

eBumps successfully launched in January of 2021 in Manhattan, and I stepped down as CTO. Since the start, I'd been clear with the CEO that my goal was to get the product to launch and then move on. I did my best to set up the new CTO, who had been our lead developer, and to stay available for any questions. I don't know all the details, but eBumps was not able to secure a second round of funding despite the apparent success of the launch. Outdoor advertising was struggling in general at this point and this could easily be attributed to the pandemic, but regardless of the cause the business gradually died off.

I spent much of 2021 helping my parents fix up my childhood home and help them sell it so that they could move further south in Maine. I spent a lot of time renovating their master bathroom and various smaller projects around the house. I also learned a lot more about the housing market and real estate, which was valuable. Later in 2021, my friend purchased a five acre plot in Western Maine. The house was lovely, but had been trashed by the previous owners. It was really interesting going right from helping my parents' prep a house for sale, to the other side of the equation where I was helping my friend fix up and get his new house more comfortable.

The property had a really well designed but pretty beat up wood shop behind the house. I was really excited to fix this place up and get a shop up and running! I ended up claiming what had been the wood storage area above the shop, cleaning it up and insulating it so that I could live there. There was no running water, but there was electricity and I had full access to the main house. It was a really cozy spot.

While here, I found a temporary job opening at Kents Hill School teaching two math classes for a semester. They seemed very relieved to have a bit of help after an instructor had left unexpectedly the previous semester. You can read more about this experience on my [education](/education/) page!

Living in my buddy's wood shop was a pretty cool experience. With the pandemic starting to wind down, or at least become normalized, I was craving more social interaction after living in the woods of Maine for a while. My friend was starting a Ph.D in Electrical Engineering at Harvard and asked if I'd be interested in finding a place with him in the area. I have a lot of friends in Cambridge and Somerville, so I was excited by the opportunity! 

This unfortuntely meant I had to turn down Kents Hill School's generous offer of a full time position, including on campus housing, and to fund a Masters in Education from the nearby University of Maine at Farmington.

I spent most of the intervening summer doing carpentry work on an island in Muscongus Bay off the coast of Maine with two friends. This was an incredibly fun but also challenging experience, you can read a bit about it on my [build](/build/) page, or in a lot more detail on this [blog post](/posts/island/).

I moved to Somerville, MA at the end of the summer. I continued doing carpentry work with the two friends from the island, mostly traveling to Vermont and Maine to work on renovation projects of varying scales. I also re-ignited my passion for music. My roommate was (and still is) a very talented drummer, so we'd have regular jam sessions and bring friends over to play.

Towards the end of my time in Somerville, I was offered a job working for Savu Sauna in Burlington, VT, which you can read more about on my [build](/build) page. Our first job was on site in the mountains in Georgia. Only a few days before flying out, I learned that I very likely had testicular cancer. That threw me for a loop! My urologist told me that given the size of my suspected tumors, there was no risk in me taking the two week trip to Georgia, and that we'd schedule my surgery for shortly after I get back.

The Georgia trip was busy and intense, which was probably a good distraction for me at this moment! The build went really well, as did my surgery after I returned home. I was lucky to have access to the excellent care of the CHA hospital system in Cambridge and Somerville, and I had state subsidized health insurance that left me miraculously with no debt after all was said and done. I'm lucky to be in excellent health now. Not the kind of thing you expect to have to deal with as a healthy and active 25-year-old, but it can happen to anyone!

After this experience, I decided to live in a van. Not the most original idea perhaps, but I'd been tempted for a while and I decided that life is precious, and that I should take the chance. I ended up getting a 2001 Westfalia Eurovan.

Savu was really happy with our work in Georgia and we established an exclusive relationship with them, building all of their saunas in their shop in Vermont. This was a great time for me to be making the transition to living in my van, as I had access to the shop with a kitchenette, bathroom, and electricity as needed. I found more and more viable parking spots in the area as time went on. I found a great deal on a solar battery and mounted some solar panels on my roof. 

The winter in my van in Vermont was challenging, but overall a nice experience. I was lucky that this winter was generally warmer than typical. I had a heated blanket, a 0 degree sleeping bag, and a couple other bags that I layered most nights as I had no safe way to run heat overnight. 

After about a year, I left this job and spent most of the summer exploring Vermont in my van. I spent some time getting to know Brattleboro and Central Vermont. I took a canoe building class at [Yestermorrow](https://yestermorrow.org/). For this class, I went down ahead of time and found some dispersed camping in the Green Mountains in Warren, about 15 minutes away from Yestermorrow. I was able to set up there for the duration of the class. This was such a lovely experience. I met some really cool people. And after a year of living in my van, I was actually able to set up outside of the van, rather than parking somewhere and just hunkering down in the van for the night to clear out in the morning. I set up an outdoor kitchen with a pop up canopy and a propane stove. There was a stream running right past the site. 

After the class, I ended up loving the Green Mountains so much that I just stayed at this spot for over 2 months. I'd travel into Burlington regularly to visit friends, and spend my time in the woods. I felt like I needed a break from the hustle and bustle of life. I did my best to follow the leave-no-trace principles I'd learned many years prior at summer camp, and cleaned up trash others had left as much as possible.

Once fall came around, the site was somehow even more beautiful than it had been. But it started to cool off, and I started to feel restless. I didn't really want to do another winter in the van, especially without more of a direction in life. So, I moved into a little apartment in the south end of Burlington!

I did some small jobs here and there for friends. Once the spring came around, I did some bigger jobs including the deck on my [build](/build/) page. I also visited my friend in Maine to help him build a cabin on his parent's property. 

I eventually felt burned out working for myself. In some ways, it was perfect. But it takes a lot of work and can be stressful to be responsible for every detail of the project from start to finish. I met a local contractor through a friend and began working with him. I was excited by the chance to work on a larger variety of projects and learn some more skills. We largely did custom high-end bathroom and kitchen renovations. Although I started as a carpenter, I quickly took over a significant amount of Project Management work, including managing client relationships and running job sites. 

This was a great opportunity for me to learn more about how to run these bigger jobs. However I eventually became frustrated with the lack of organization and the cash flow issues the business faced, and I realized that unfortunately I could not continue with that contractor. 

Since then, I have continued to do smaller jobs on my own, and have done some finish work for another local contractor. I am also returning to my passion for education, and have been tutoring math and science as well.
