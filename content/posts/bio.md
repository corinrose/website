In 2016 as a senior at the Maine School of Science and Mathematics, I took a Computational Biology class in partnership with the [Jackson Laboratory](https://www.jax.org/) in Bar Harbor, Maine. This is where I first learned how much I love taking complex ideas and figuring out how to express them visually.

This class was intended to expose us to the reality of scientific research. We utilized a dataset from Alan D. Attie's Lab at the University of Wisconsin-Madison. This dataset included 144 measured phenotypes, as well as detailed analysis of their genomes.

Using RStudio to perform our analysis, we learned about QTL (quantitative trait loci) analysis, which uses statistical analysis to link physical locations on a chromosome (QTLs) with the expression of certain phenotypes. Once we identified QTLs of interest, we used methods such as Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) to determine the most likely causal relationship between these genes and phenotypes.

We were able to establish a likely relationship between Ell3, a gene on chromosome 2; the expression of autophagy, a process where our bodies break down cells to reuse old or damaged parts; and the presence of phenotypes exhibited in Type 2 Diabetes, namely insulin levels. While this research does not provide any conclusive next steps, it does build on existing research correlating autophagy with Type 2 Diabetes, and clarifies the complex relationship between autophagy, insulin resistance, and the expression of gene Ell3.
